The problem

The platform that was meant to speed you up is slowing you down.

Over-customised. Nervously upgraded. Owned by nobody. The business works around it while run costs climb and the pace of change falls. We see five problems again and again, and two of them cause the other three.

Poor core platform health Cause

Years of customisation debt and sprawl that make every change harder than the last.

Legacy ways of running SaaS Cause

Project-era governance applied to a platform that's meant to evolve every release.

Slow delivery Symptom

Demand piles up, upgrades become programmes, and the business stops asking.

Manual ways of working Symptom

Testing, releases and admin done by hand, so effort scales with headcount.

Too much reliance on people Symptom

Key-person risk everywhere, and a platform only a few people dare to touch.

The insight
"Should we replace it?" Usually, no. It's rarely the platform's fault. It's how it's owned, governed and changed. Fix how you run it.

A replacement programme is expensive, slow and risky, and if the operating model doesn't change, the same debt rebuilds on the new platform within a few years. We tell you which problem you actually have before you spend on the wrong answer.

Global top-50 services & technology provider

From fragmented teams, tools and platforms to one employee experience

The problem: employee journeys split across ServiceNow, Microsoft, Atlassian and new AI tools, run by fragmented teams. Technology sprawl, an inconsistent experience, and change that took too long to land.

What changed: one employee experience, with idea-to-value cut from 6–8 weeks to 10 days and millions in committed spend put to work. Backlog and demand management automated. Releases got smaller, faster, more frequent and higher quality.

Fortune 500 logistics company

From bureaucratic releases to rapid, compliant, high-quality delivery

The problem: a very large centre of excellence and multiple partners, held back by manual processes and inspection, fragmented toolchains and error-prone deployments. Releases had become slow and complicated.

What changed: a highly automated pipeline and a redefined definition of done. Testing and quality moved into the sprint, with automated code checks built in.

  • 2,000+Hours a week repurposed
  • +90%Automated testing
  • −70%Deployment and upgrade time decrease
  • −50%Partner spend decrease
Global top-10 IT services provider · HCM

From disconnected employee and candidate experiences to moments that matter

The problem: years of acquisitions, poor tool choices, vendor lock-in and a sprawling estate. The result was tribal knowledge, little integration, and a broken flow of work and ways of working.

What changed: standard ways of working built around employees, with consistent engagement points across the whole employee journey. Standardisation and modernisation took the friction out.
